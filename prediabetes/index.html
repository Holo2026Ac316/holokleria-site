How to use them

These twelve meals show the plate from the book in real food. They are worked examples, not a menu. Cook the ones that sound good, change what you like, and skip the rest. Each meal serves one or two, or makes enough to eat twice, and most use frozen or canned vegetables somewhere, because they keep for months and cost less than fresh ones that wilt before you get to them.

Start with a meal that sounds good and the shopping page. Print the blank week when you want to plan your own, and print the shopping page as often as you need it. You do not need every page.

You may print or photocopy these pages for your own personal, noncommercial use.

These pages are for general education and do not replace advice from your own clinician. If you have been given a special eating plan, for your kidneys or anything else, follow it first.

The numbers in these pages are close estimates from the U.S. Department of Agriculture's FoodData Central database. Yours will differ with brands and with how much you use. Read them as a guide rather than a label. These pages offer general information and are not medical advice for any one person. Never start, stop, or change a prescription medicine on your own because of something you read here.
