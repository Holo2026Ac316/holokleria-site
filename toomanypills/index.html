How to use them

Keep one set for each person whose medicines you keep track of. If you help two people, keep two. You don't need every page. Start with the complete medicine list and keep it in front, with a log of changes behind it. Add other pages only when a problem or a change calls for them. Each tool is explained in the book, in the chapter listed beside it. The contents page in the PDF shows where each tool starts, so you can print just the pages you need.

You may print or photocopy these pages for your own use or for someone you help.

One rule runs through all of them: these pages help you notice, ask, and plan. None of them is a reason to stop or change a prescription medicine on your own.

These pages offer general information about medicines and older adults. They are not medical advice for any one person and do not replace the care of a clinician or pharmacist who knows your history. Never stop or change a prescription medicine on your own because of something you read here.
